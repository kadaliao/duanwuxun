SUBJECT: The elderly temple keeper (庙祝) of a small Dragon King temple on a hill, about 65: thin, calm, kindly face with white eyebrows and a wispy white beard, wears a plain grey Daoist-style robe and a soft cloth cap; holds a bundle of incense sticks; hands together.
COMPOSITION: half-length character portrait (绣像) as in the portrait pages of late-Ming illustrated novels; figure centered, from the waist up, three-quarter view, on bare plain paper background with absolutely nothing else, generous empty paper margin on all sides of the figure.
STYLE: an authentic Chinese woodblock print (木刻版画) book illustration in the manner of the finest late-Ming Huizhou-school prints (徽派版画, Wanli–Chongzhen era) and the figure drawing of Chen Hongshou (陈洪绶). Every form is defined by bold yet elegant carved contour lines; long, rhythmic, flowing drapery lines; faces drawn with very few fine lines and NO shading on faces; flat solid black carved areas for black gauze caps, lacquered furniture and deep night; only sparse parallel hatching where needed; water, clouds, rain, snow, smoke and flames drawn as stylized decorative line motifs. NO Western engraving cross-hatching, NO chiaroscuro, NO cast shadows, NO tonal gradients, NO photographic realism. Architecture in ruled-line (界画) parallel perspective. Printed in black ink on warm aged cream xuan paper with visible fiber texture and the slight unevenness of hand printing. Monochrome black ink only unless an accent color is explicitly specified. Historically accurate mid-16th-century Ming dynasty costume and architecture. Absolutely NO text, NO calligraphy, NO Chinese characters, NO seals, NO signatures, NO watermark, NO border frame.
